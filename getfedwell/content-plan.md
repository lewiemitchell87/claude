# Get Fed Well — Content Plan & Strategy

A practical roadmap for getting getfedwell.com off the ground with content that
helps real families eat with fewer ultra-processed foods (UPFs) — and that
search engines can actually find.

---

## 1. What we're trying to be

**The promise:** Get Fed Well helps busy families cut ultra-processed food out of
everyday meals without spending all weekend in the kitchen or all their money in
the health-food aisle. Realistic, affordable, UK-supermarket food — not
perfectionism.

**Who it's for (start narrow, it ranks better):**
- Parents who've read/heard about UPF and want to do something but feel overwhelmed.
- People who tried "clean eating" and bounced off because it was expensive or fussy.
- Anyone Googling "is [X] ultra-processed?" — that's huge, low-competition traffic.

**Brand voice:** Warm, honest, non-preachy. We are *not* the food police. We eat
real life. "Better, not perfect" is the whole ethos.

---

## 2. Site structure (the pages WordPress needs)

Foundational pages to publish first — these build trust and are linked from every post:

- **Home** — one-line promise + featured posts + email sign-up.
- **About / Our Story** — you and your wife, why you started this. People follow people.
- **Start Here** — a guided intro for newcomers ("New to non-UPF? Read these 3 posts").
- **What is UPF?** — the cornerstone explainer (drafted: `posts/what-is-ultra-processed-food.md`).
- **Recipes** — index, filterable by meal/time.
- **Contact** + basic legal (Privacy Policy, affiliate disclosure if you monetise).

---

## 3. The content engine: three post types

1. **Pillar / explainer posts** — evergreen, answer big questions, internally link to everything.
   _e.g. "What is ultra-processed food?", "Is bread ultra-processed?"_
2. **Recipes** — the bread and butter (literally). Quick, family-friendly, UK ingredients.
3. **"Swap this for that" / shopping posts** — high intent, easy to monetise later.
   _e.g. "7 non-UPF supermarket snacks for kids", "Non-UPF bread brands in UK supermarkets"_

**Golden rule for SEO:** each post should answer one specific question a real person
types into Google. Specific beats clever every time.

## 5. SEO basics that punch above their weight

- **One target phrase per post**, used in the title, the URL slug, the first paragraph, and one heading.
- **Write the meta description** (≈155 chars) for every post — it's the advert in Google results.
- **Internal links:** every recipe links to the relevant pillar post ("not sure what counts as UPF? read this"), and pillars link to recipes. This is the single biggest free SEO win.
- **Real photos** beat stock — phone photos of your actual food rank and convert better.
- **FAQ section** at the bottom of pillar posts (great for Google's "People also ask").

---

## 6. Beyond the blog (when you have bandwidth)

- **Email list from day one** — a free "10 easy non-UPF swaps" PDF in exchange for an email. Your most valuable asset; you own it, unlike social followers.
- **One social channel, done well** (Instagram or a Facebook group fits this audience) rather than spreading thin.
- **Monetisation later:** affiliate links to ingredients/equipment, sponsored recipes, an eventual e-book or meal-plan product. Build the audience first.
